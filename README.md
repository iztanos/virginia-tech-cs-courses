# Virginia Tech CS Courses

A data-backed guide to every Virginia Tech Computer Science course from **CS 1014 to CS 4994** — 73 courses —
scored on two separate questions:

- **How useful is it?** Career payoff: what it unlocks, how often the skill gets used, interview relevance.
- **How well is it taught?** Synthesized from public grade distributions, public instructor ratings, and student comments.

These are deliberately kept apart. They correlate less than you'd expect.

> **Data vintage:** grade distributions **AY 2019-20 → 2021-22** · instructor ratings **through Aug 2026** · catalog **2026-27**.
> Read [docs/methodology.md](docs/methodology.md) before drawing conclusions from any single number.

## Degree structure (2026-27 catalog, 123 credits)

- **Degree core:** CS 1114, 2505, 2506, 3214, 3604 — C or better required on the 2000-level ones
- **Major requirements:** CS 2104, CS 3304, one Theory Elective, one CS 4/5XXX Elective, one Capstone
- **Additional:** CS 1944 (1cr), CS 2114, CS 4944 (1cr), MATH 3134 or MATH 3124
- **Electives:** 2× CS 3/4/5XXX + 1× CS Technical Elective
- **Theory Elective set:** CS 4104, 4114, 4124, 4134 (plus graduate options CS 5104, 5114)
- **Capstone set:** CS 4094, 4274, 4284, 4624, 4634, 4644, 4664, 4704, 4784, 4884
- **Capstone gate (Fall 2025 onward):** passing grades in **CS 2506 and CS 3114** required to enroll
- **CS 3114** carries Pathways 5A but **cannot** double-count with any other requirement
- **CS 3900 Bridge Experience** (0cr) — satisfied by internship, co-op, research, or study abroad

## How to read the scores

**Usefulness** is career payoff, not intellectual merit. CS 4134 Quantum Computation is fascinating and
scores a 3, because almost no graduating senior will use it in the next five years. Required courses are
scored on what they unlock, not on whether you have a choice.

**Teaching** blends four things: RateMyProfessors quality and would-take-again, withdrawal rate, the size of
the spread between instructors, and recurring themes in the free-text comments. A course where every section
is fine scores better than a course with one great instructor and one disastrous one, even at the same mean.

**What the numbers cannot tell you:** a low GPA does not mean a course is badly taught. CS 3214 has one of
the lowest GPAs in the major and is the best-run course in the department — its 4.4% withdrawal rate, in the
hardest course students take, is the tell. Conversely CS 3604 posts a 3.79 GPA and perfect instructor ratings,
and students say plainly that they learned nothing.

## Contributing

Corrections welcome, especially from anyone with newer data. The grade export used here stops at Spring 2022;
if you have access to a more recent VT Institutional Research extract, that is the single highest-value
contribution. See [docs/methodology.md](docs/methodology.md) for how the current numbers were produced.

This guide reports public data — VT's published grade distributions and public RateMyProfessors ratings —
with sample sizes attached. Instructor names appear because they are the most actionable variable in the
whole dataset. Nothing here is a personal judgment of anyone; it is what the public record says, and small
samples are labeled as such.
