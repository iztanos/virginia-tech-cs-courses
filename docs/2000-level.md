# 2000-level courses

[← back to index](../README.md) · [1000](1000-level.md) · [3000](3000-level.md) · [4000](4000-level.md)

Eleven courses. Three of them — CS 2114, 2505, 2506 — are the spine of the degree and the hardest block
most students hit before CS 3214. CS 2506 posts the second-lowest GPA of any required course.

---

### CS 2064: Intermediate Programming in Python

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · Pathways 5A + 10**
**Prerequisites:** CS 1064

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.06 | 2.0% | 98 | 2.40 (n=30) | 3.13 |

Advanced control flow and data processing, data structures, computational techniques, object-oriented
programming, and modern data science pipelines — implementation in data science and production
environments.

Aimed at scientists and engineers. It is an accepted alternative prerequisite into CS 2114, which is its
main structural role. Ratings are mediocre across 30 reviews. CS majors have no reason to be here.

> **Usefulness 3/10 · Teaching C−**

### CS 2104: Introduction to Problem Solving in Computer Science

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · MAJOR REQUIREMENT**
**Prerequisites:** none stated

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.55 | 1.3% | 1,404 | 3.58 (n=26) | 2.19 |

A broad range of heuristics for solving problems relevant to computation: classical math and word problems,
generating solutions to real-life professional problems, and problem solving through computation.

Genuinely divisive, and the split is instructor-shaped. **Patrick Sullivan** and **Sehrish Nizamani** draw
strong recent reviews — "one of the best CS professors I've ever had," "very helpful and very clearly cares
about her students." **Margaret Ellis** polarizes within the same semester: one 2026 review calls the class
"completely meaningless" with an "absurd amount of classwork," another calls her "amazing... genuinely cares
a lot about her students."

Low difficulty, easy grade, thin skill transfer either way.

> **Usefulness 4/10 · Teaching B**

### CS 2114: Software Design and Data Structures

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · REQUIRED · Pathways 6D + 10**
**Prerequisites:** CS 1114 or CS 2064

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.20 | 3.5% | **2,675** | 2.50 (n=97) | 3.19 |

Programming-intensive exploration of software design: advanced object-oriented design, ethics in computing,
algorithm development and analysis, classic data structures, and a team-based software project.

The real on-ramp to the major. Every section runs identical content and format — as one student put it,
"all profs teach the class with identical format + content, so whoever your prof is shouldn't make a big
diff in your grade." The data mostly agrees, with one exception:

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Farghally** | **3.50** | 1% | 279 |
| Esakia | 3.24 | 1% | 229 |
| Haqq | 3.23 | 2% | 944 |
| Ellis | 3.10 | 5% | 1,223 |

**Mohammed Farghally** is the clear pick and draws consistent 4–5/5 reviews ("nice guy who wants you to
learn, and he's very accessible"). Web-CAT frustration dominates the negative comments again, along with
slow TA grading — one 2026 review describes a grade drifting from an A to a B−/C+ over four weeks as
assignments were marked.

> **Usefulness 9/10 · Teaching C — the content is fine; the autograder experience drags it down**

### CS 2144: Competitive Problem Solving I

**3 credits**
**Prerequisites:** CS 2114

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3.80 | 0.0% | 10 |

Algorithms, data structures, and implementation techniques taught through judged competitive programming.
Arrays, lists, maps, trees; recursion, divide-and-conquer, dynamic programming, search and traversal, graph
representations, computational geometry; macro and micro optimization.

Formalized out of the old CS 2984 special study (GPA 3.54, n=236 in the sample window). **This is the most
directly interview-shaped course VT offers** — the judged-submission format is LeetCode and ICPC practice,
for credit, with a structure around it.

The enrollment is tiny and self-selecting, so the 3.80 GPA is not a difficulty signal.

> **Usefulness 6/10 generally, 8/10 if you are grinding technical interviews · Teaching n/d**

### CS 2164: Foundations of Contemporary Security Environments

**3 credits · Fall, Spring**
**Prerequisites:** none stated

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 3.62 | 3.1% | 217 | 4.00 (n=4, thin) |

Political, legal, ethical, technical, environmental, historical, and cultural perspectives on contemporary
security environments; applying and analyzing the effectiveness of security procedures, tools, and policies;
risk and decision-making.

Part of the Secure Computing major's foundation. This is a **policy and framing course, not a technical
one** — no exploitation, no cryptographic implementation, no tooling. Useful context, minimal skill
transfer.

> **Usefulness 3/10 · Teaching B (thin data)**

### CS 2304: Topics in Programming Systems

**1 credit · Fall, Spring · repeatable 3× (max 2× for major credit)**
**Prerequisites:** CS 2114

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.64 | **7.9%** | 406 | 5.00 (n=4, thin) | 1.75 |

Guided self-study in one specific programming system — JavaScript, C++, CUDA, Ruby, SQL, FORTRAN, UNIX —
covering its syntax and applications.

An underrated way to pick up a concrete, marketable skill for a single credit. The SQL, JavaScript, and CUDA
variants are the ones with real career return.

One caution the numbers make plain: a **7.9% withdrawal rate against a 1.75 difficulty rating**. Nothing
about the material is hard. It is self-paced, and self-paced is where people quietly disappear. Set your own
deadlines in week one.

> **Usefulness 5/10 — cheap, targeted, real · Teaching B+**

### CS 2505: Computer Organization I

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · DEGREE CORE**
**Prerequisites:** CS 1114 or CS 2064 (C or better) · corequisite MATH 2534 or MATH 3034

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2.95 | 8.7% | 1,798 | 3.37 (n=63) | 3.43 |

Design and operation of digital computers, working up from the logic gate level: combinational and
sequential circuits, information representation, computer arithmetic, ALUs, control unit design, basic
computer organization, and the relationship between high-level languages and instruction set architectures.
Heavy C programming throughout.

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tausif | 3.78 | 1% | 122 |
| Wenskovitch | 3.57 | 1% | 75 |
| McPherson | 2.94 | 9% | 798 |
| McQuain | 2.81 | 10% | 678 |
| Paul | 2.63 | 14% | 125 |

**Allyson Senger** dominates recent reviews and splits sharply. The praise is specific and repeated — caring,
responsive, supportive, "for such a difficult class, she makes it a whole lot easier," "she responds quickly
and makes you feel supported, not judged." The criticism is equally specific: a final exam covering material
barely addressed during the semester. **David McPherson** draws consistent praise for passion and elaboration.

For most students this is the first serious encounter with C and assembly. It is a step change from CS 2114.

> **Usefulness 9/10 · Teaching B−**

### CS 2506: Computer Organization II

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · DEGREE CORE**
**Prerequisites:** (CS 2114 or ECE 3514) and (CS 2505 or ECE 2564) and (MATH 2534 or MATH 3034), all C or better

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| **2.68** | 9.3% | 1,261 | 3.16 (n=28) | **4.18** |

Instruction formats and construction, addressing modes, instruction execution, **memory hierarchy operation
and performance**, pipelining, input/output, and the relationship between high-level languages and machine
language.

**Second-lowest GPA in the required core**, and since Fall 2025 a hard gate for the capstone. This is where
cache behavior and pipelining stop being trivia and start explaining why your code is slow — the direct
feeder into CS 3214.

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Feng | 2.86 | 11% | 108 |
| Nikolopoulos | 2.78 | 5% | 335 |
| McQuain | 2.75 | **17%** | 291 |
| Wenskovitch | 2.64 | 10% | 106 |
| Jian | 2.52 | 6% | 421 |

**Dimitrios Nikolopoulos** is the interesting case. He runs Discord office hours covering most of the day and
records supplemental lectures, and students single that out as what makes the course survivable — while
simultaneously noting visible frustration in lecture when students don't follow. Reviews of **Senger**'s
final here are unusually bleak.

> **Usefulness 9/10 · Teaching B−**
