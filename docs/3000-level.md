# 3000-level courses

[← back to index](../README.md) · [1000](1000-level.md) · [2000](2000-level.md) · [4000](4000-level.md)

Nineteen entries. This is where the major happens: CS 3114 and CS 3214 are the two highest-usefulness
courses in the entire catalog, and most of the elective branches open from here.

### CS 3114: Data Structures and Algorithms

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · REQUIRED · Pathways 5A**
**Prerequisites:** (CS 2114 or ECE 3514) and (CS 2505 or ECE 2564) and (MATH 2534 or MATH 3034)

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3.00 | 8.0% | 1,536 | 3.05 (n=42) | 3.67 | 96% |

Advanced data structures and performance analysis: sorting, searching, hashing, advanced tree structures and
algorithms, file system organization and access methods, plus ethical issues in data analysis and software
performance. Projects demand real design and implementation work.

Interview currency, prerequisite for nearly every worthwhile elective, and since Fall 2025 a capstone gate.
Note that despite carrying Pathways 5A it **cannot double-count** with any other requirement — the only
course in the degree with that restriction.

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Edmison | **3.72** | 1% | 135 |
| Cao | 3.11 | 6% | 546 |
| Sullivan | 2.98 | **16%** | 207 |
| McQuain | 2.73 | 9% | 552 |
| Davis | 2.62 | 6% | 62 |

A 1.10 GPA spread across sections of the same required course. **Mohammed Farghally** draws the best recent
reviews — "by far the best professor I've had at VT... good lecturer and gives a lot of leeway." **Yang Cao**
draws the worst: a Spring 2025 review reports a section starting at 150 students and dropping to 40 before
the drop deadline. **Patrick Sullivan** is genuinely split — praised for being approachable and giving clear
answers, criticized in the same term for leaving a midterm ungraded for six weeks.

The standing student advice for the projects: start early, choose your partner carefully, go to office hours
the moment you're stuck.

> **Usefulness 10/10 · Teaching C+ · The section matters more here than anywhere else in the degree**

### CS 3214: Computer Systems

**3 credits · Fall, Spring · DEGREE CORE**
**Prerequisites:** CS 2506 and CS 3114

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 2.92 | **4.4%** | 1,077 | 3.63 (n=23) | **4.48** | 94% |

Computer systems as they matter to application programmers, with emphasis on operating system principles:
processes, threads, synchronization, deadlock, CPU scheduling, system call interfaces, system-level I/O and
file management, shell programming, separate compilation, loading and linking, IPC, virtual and physical
memory management, garbage collection, and network programming.

**The highest-value course in the catalog.** Four large C projects — shell, malloc, threadpool, web server —
plus exercises. Students report 20+ hours a week for an A and a midterm average around 40%.

The number that matters most is the withdrawal rate: **4.4%, in the hardest course in the major.** People
finish this course. That is what a well-built hard course looks like — real autograders, a maintained public
course site, genuinely scaffolded projects.

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Williams | 2.99 | 3% | 96 |
| Butt | 2.98 | 7% | 215 |
| Hu | 2.93 | 6% | 82 |
| Back | 2.92 | 4% | 451 |
| McQuain | 2.84 | 2% | 183 |
| Hicks | 2.78 | 4% | 50 |

Grades are remarkably consistent across instructors — this is run as one course, not six.

**Dan Williams** gets the strongest recent reviews: "an incredible professor... made all the lecture material
clear," "he was nice, knowledgeable, and wanted us to succeed." **Godmar Back** designed the course and
polarizes hard — one 2026 review praises him for teaching in a way that makes you "learn the material well,"
another from the same academic year calls his lectures unfollowable: "he is flying through a Linux terminal
typing at 100 wpm and you can barely follow along."

Worth knowing regardless of your section: multiple students in *other* instructors' sections report studying
exclusively from Back's recorded lectures and finishing ~20 points above the class average.

> **Usefulness 10/10 · Teaching B+ · If you take one hard course seriously, make it this one**

### CS 3274: Software Reverse Engineering

**3 credits · Spring only**
**Prerequisites:** (CS 2114 and CS 2506) or (ECE 2534 and ECE 3514)

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2.85 | **18.8%** | 52 |

Theory and practice of software reverse engineering: static and dynamic analysis techniques and tools,
reverse engineering of malware and obfuscated binaries, communications and command-and-control analysis,
and reverse engineering of non-binary software.

**The highest withdrawal rate of any CS course in the dataset** — nearly one student in five. Excellent,
genuinely differentiated preparation for security work, and a bad idea if security is not your direction.

> **Usefulness 7/10 for security careers, 3/10 otherwise · Teaching n/d**

### CS 3304: Comparative Languages

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · MAJOR REQUIREMENT**
**Prerequisites:** CS 3114 (C or better)

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.24 | 4.0% | 1,043 | 2.33 (n=6, thin) | 4.17 |

Programming language constructs with emphasis on run-time behavior, studied two ways: the fundamental
elements of languages and their inclusion in commercial systems, and the differences between implementations
of common elements across languages.

The content genuinely broadens how you think about languages. The execution draws fire, and the criticism
appears even inside the positive reviews: exams reward memorizing the syntax of languages you will never use
again.

### CS 3314: Programming Language Theory and Practice

**3 credits · Fall only**
**Prerequisites:** CS 3114

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP | RMP difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---:|
| no data (new) | — | — | 4.20 (n=5) | 2.60 |

Theoretical basis of programming languages: formal languages, computability theory, type theory, language

A newer course with encouraging early ratings — 4.20 quality against a 2.60 difficulty is a strong opening

### CS 3604: Professionalism in Computing

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · DEGREE CORE**
**Prerequisites:** none stated · prerequisite for CS 4944

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| **3.79** | 0.5% | 1,250 | **5.00 (n=9)** | **1.11** |

Ethical, social, and professional concerns of the computing field: social impact of computing, effects on
society, the responsibilities of computing professionals, case studies, and an overview of computing history.

**The most honest paradox in the catalog.** Perfect instructor ratings, a 3.79 GPA, a 1.11 difficulty rating
— and students say the quiet part out loud: *"Class was extremely easy — probably spent 15 total hours on the
class and got an A... Don't take Prof Dunlap if you want to learn something."*

**Daniel Dunlap** is genuinely well-liked — chill, engaging, forgiving on late work, visibly invested in his
students. The course is reading quizzes, discussion posts, essays, and a partnered case-study presentation.

Take him, take the three credits, and don't expect the course to change you.

> **Usefulness 2/10 · Teaching A as an experience, F as skill-building**

### CS 3634: CS Foundations for Computational Modeling & Data Analytics

**3 credits · Fall, Spring**
**Prerequisites:** see catalog — CMDA-track

No CS-major grade data.

Survey of the CS concepts and tools that enable computational science and data analytics: data structure
design and implementation, performance analysis, high-performance architectures, parallel computation, and
the operating system concepts that affect large-scale analytics performance.

**Built for CMDA majors, not CS majors.** It is a compressed substitute for CS 3114 and CS 3214 material.
As a CS major you take the real courses instead.

> **Usefulness 3/10 for CS majors · Teaching n/d**

Software design principles with emphasis on engineering practice: lifecycle activities including design,

### CS 3714: Mobile Software Development

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer**
**Prerequisites:** CS 3114 or equivalent

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.41 | 6.7% | 498 | 2.27 (n=15) | 3.60 |

Technologies and concepts underlying mobile development: mobile platforms, architecture, OS and programming
environment; design patterns for mobile applications; network-centric development; data persistence;
programming for cameras, recorders, accelerometers, gyroscopes, and antennas; location-aware development.

The content is reasonable. The delivery is the problem. **Osman Balci** teaches most sections and has six
consecutive 1/5 ratings running through 2026 — "worst professor in the vt cs department in my opinion, his
lectures are so out of date and his office hours were never helpful," alongside repeated accounts of students
being ridiculed for asking questions. The dissenting reviews describe him as blunt and strict but
knowledgeable.

**Esakia** 3.72 GPA versus Balci 3.33 in the same course.

> **Usefulness 5/10 · Teaching D+ · Only worth it with Esakia or a new instructor**

### CS 3724: Introduction to Human-Computer Interaction

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer**
**Prerequisites:** CS 2114 or equivalent · gateway to CS 4634, 4644, 4774, 4784

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3.57 | **0.5%** | 562 | 2.82 (n=17) | 3.41 | 100% |

Survey of HCI concepts, theory, and practice: the basic components of human-computer interaction and its
interdisciplinary underpinnings; critical evaluation of computer-based technology from a user-oriented rather
than system-oriented perspective, across both human (cognitive, social) and technological (I/O, interaction
styles, devices) thrusts; design guidelines, evaluation methods, participatory design.

Easy, well-liked, and the gateway to an entire branch of the catalog. **D. Scott McCrickard** posts a 3.91
GPA with 0% withdrawals across 175 students. **Amun Kharel** collects repeated 5/5 reviews — "always has
engaging lectures and is passionate about what he teaches," "a very reasonable grader, flexible with
extensions."

**Andrew Kulak** is the weak point in the ratings, though even a defender's review concedes only that he
"grades kind of tough and can come off as a little arrogant" while finding the class interesting.

> **Usefulness 6/10 generally, 9/10 for product or design careers · Teaching A−**

### CS 3744: Introduction to GUI Programming and Graphics

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer**
**Prerequisites:** CS 3114 and MATH 2114

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.44 | 6.7% | 402 | **1.20 (n=5)** | 3.80 |

Design and implementation of object-oriented graphical user interfaces and 2D graphics systems: callbacks,
handlers, event listeners, design patterns, layout managers, architectural models; and the mathematical
foundations of graphics — clipping, scan conversion, affine and convex linear transformations, projections,
viewing, structuring.

**The lowest course-level rating in the department at n≥5.** The graphics-math half is legitimate; the GUI
half is dated desktop-toolkit material that transfers poorly to modern frontend work.

If you want graphics, take CS 4204. If you want interface work, take CS 3724.

> **Usefulness 3/10 · Teaching D**

### CS 3900: Bridge Experience

**0 credits · REQUIRED**
**Prerequisites:** departmental approval of your 3900 plan

Application of academic knowledge in a work-based experience aligned with post-graduation goals — typically
an internship, co-op, undergraduate research, or study abroad — plus self-evaluation, reflection, and a
showcase of learning.

Zero credits and mandatory. Plan it early; the approval step is what catches people late.
