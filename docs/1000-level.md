# 1000-level courses

[← back to index](../README.md) · [2000](2000-level.md) · [3000](3000-level.md) · [4000](4000-level.md)

Six courses. Only one — CS 1114 — is part of the CS major core; the rest are service courses for other
majors or one-credit administration. If you are a CS major, the entire useful content of this page is
CS 1114 and CS 1944.

---

### CS 1014: Introduction to Computational Thinking

**3 credits · Fall, Spring · Pathways 5F + 10**
**Prerequisites:** none

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 3.23 | 3.2% | 548 | 3.11 (n=9) | 3.44 |

Computational thinking as a perspective rather than a skill — problem formulation, applying computational
tools to large-scale problems across domains, a basic introduction to algorithms and one practical
language, plus the societal and ethical implications of computational systems.

Built for non-majors who want computational literacy without committing to programming. Perfectly
reasonable at what it does. CS majors skip it entirely.

> **Usefulness 2/10 · Teaching C+**

---

### CS 1044: Introduction to Programming in C

**3 credits · Spring only**
**Prerequisites:** none · partially duplicates CS 1344

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3.24 | 2.3% | 388 | 2.04 (n=25) | 3.84 | **44%** |

Structured programming in C: statement sequencing, logic control, structured data, input/output, functions.

A legacy service course for engineering majors. The 44% would-take-again rate is among the worst in the
department, and it sits alongside a *low* difficulty rating — which says the frustration is with delivery,
not with rigor. CS majors belong in CS 1114, and will learn substantially more C in CS 2505 anyway.

> **Usefulness 2/10 · Teaching D**

---

### CS 1054: Introduction to Programming in Java

**3 credits · Fall only · Pathways 5F + 11**
**Prerequisites:** none

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 2.96 | 4.3% | 513 | 1.92 (n=19) | 4.21 | **50%** |

Introductory object-oriented programming in Java: control flow, classes, objects, methods, instantiation,
message passing, plus the impact of computing on diversity and inclusion.

The lowest-rated introductory course at VT. Unlike CS 1044, it is also genuinely hard for what it is — a
4.21 difficulty rating on an intro course is a pacing red flag, and the sub-3.0 GPA backs that up.
Superseded by CS 1114 for majors.

> **Usefulness 2/10 · Teaching D**

---

### CS 1064: Introduction to Programming in Python

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · Pathways 5F + 10**
**Prerequisites:** none

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 3.63 | 0.9% | **3,494** | **4.22 (n=88)** | 2.49 | **97%** |

Python fundamentals framed around scientific and engineering problems: control flow, state tracing, simple
and complex types, functional and object-oriented organization, data processing, debugging.

**The best-rated large course in the department**, and it is essentially one professor. **John Lewis**
collects sustained 4–5/5 ratings across dozens of reviews — "best professor I have had at Virginia Tech"
recurs verbatim. Students describe clear explanations, generous deadlines, and consistent accessibility.

Not a CS-major course. But if you are an engineering or science student who needs exactly one programming
course, this is the one, and Lewis is the reason.

> **Usefulness 3/10 for CS majors, 8/10 for everyone else · Teaching A**

### CS 1114: Introduction to Software Design

**3 credits · Fall, Spring, Summer · DEGREE CORE**
**Prerequisites:** none · corequisite MATH 1225 · partially duplicates CS 1054, 1124, 1705

| Avg GPA | Withdraw | Enrolled | RMP quality | RMP difficulty | Would take again |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **2.60** | **15.8%** | 2,107 | 2.64 (n=121) | 3.88 | **56%** |

Object-oriented programming fundamentals, basic software engineering principles, simple data types, control
structures, arrays and strings, basic algorithms, testing and debugging, and a model of the computer as an
abstract machine.

**The worst student experience of any required course, on every metric available.** Lowest
would-take-again in the core, second-lowest GPA, and a 15.8% withdrawal rate — roughly one student in six
does not finish.

The instructor spread is the story:

| Instructor | GPA | Withdraw | n |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Kotut | 3.04 | 1% | 138 |
| Feng | 2.81 | 12% | 104 |
| Senger | 2.78 | 19% | 109 |
| Irwin | 2.66 | 11% | 121 |
| Barnette | 2.57 | 13% | 1,368 |
| **Edwards** | **2.35** | **34%** | 267 |

A third of students in Edwards' sections withdrew. Recent bright spot: **David McPherson** collects repeated
5/5 ratings through 2026 ("class and projects were easy... he is great").

The dominant complaint across CS 1114 and CS 2114 is **Web-CAT**, VT's automated grader — finicky submission
behavior and steep late penalties appear in review after review. Budget time for submission mechanics, not
just for the code.

> **Usefulness 9/10 — unavoidable, and it gates the entire major · Teaching C− · Shop hard for the section**

### CS 1944: Computer Science First Year Seminar

**1 credit · Fall, Summer · REQUIRED**
**Prerequisites:** CS 1114 or CS 2064 or ECE 2514

No meaningful grade or rating data. Academic and career planning for CS majors.

A one-credit administrative requirement. Show up, get the credit.

> **Usefulness 1/10 · Teaching n/d**
